# AI Engineer: working on this repo

A Factorio 2.0 mod plus a helper app that lets Claude play as a second engineer. It replaces the earlier `factorio-ai-companion` project, which has been deleted. There's no dashboard: the only interface is in game.

## Layout

- `mod/`: the Factorio mod `ai-engineer`.
  - `control.lua`: commands and event wiring.
  - `scripts/bridge.lua`: the RCON protocol, pairing, owner isolation.
  - `scripts/gui.lua`: the owner's chat window. Factorio can't drag-resize windows, so title bar buttons step through `SIZES` (rebuilding the window). New lines scroll the log on the next tick (`M.tick`; their height isn't known before), except while the mouse is over the log (hover events; the labels ignore the mouse so they count as the log), since the player may have scrolled up: for at most `HOVER_HOLD` (10 s) from the mouse coming in, since a mouse parked over the window never sends a leave event and the log stopped following. The held scroll happens when the mouse leaves, and sending or clicking the input clears the hover. The title bar's status light (`LIGHTS`, entity status sprites, set in `M.tick` every 15 ticks): red when the engineer is shooting, dead or the base was attacked in the last 30 s, yellow while the app's status says Thinking, green with a job, crafting or "Working", else blue; beside it only the cost from the app's status text. The game test can't open GUIs (no players), so check GUI changes in a real game.
  - `scripts/tools.lua`: what the AI can do. Tool results stay in the conversation and are resent with every request, so keep them compact. For example, `observe` details only machines with something to report and counts plain structures. It also counts every kind of building out of its radius, with the nearest of each (`buildings_further_away`, within 256 tiles), so the AI remembers what's built (a nearest-15 list left a far power plant out, and the AI built a second one). Finished items in furnace and assembler outputs are summed up as `ready_to_take`, since small models smelt more instead of collecting. Failures say how to recover: `craft` says how to get each missing ingredient (first: "N are ready in machines: take them from …"), and `place` refuses a spot where the same building already stands. `insert` refuses any fuel but coal for mining drills (a coal drill holding wood can't store what it mines; wood is kept for poles), matching the early-game plan in `tips.md`.
  - `scripts/jobs.lua`: long-running work, run every tick. `place` chops trees and rocks in the way first (`clear_nature`, half a second each), so every spot check (`util.can_build`, used by autopilot, power and cells) counts them as cleared: in a forest, power growth found no row that fit. `mine` of an ore notes when its plates are already waiting in furnaces. A mine job lets go of mining for a tick whenever it changes target: a character still "mining" a finished rock can't select the next thing and never mines again.
  - `scripts/autopilot.lua`: keep-busy's build order (see Keep-busy mode below).
  - `scripts/power.lua`: finding water and the pump/boiler/engine layout by it.
  - The `items` tool lists the game's items (every mod's) by group, or by search with player words (`ITEM_WORDS`: "weapon" finds guns, ammo, capsules and armor), so the AI gets exact names, e.g. for cheat_items. The whole list isn't in the prompt: it's long, and mods change it.
  - `scripts/plan.lua`: the `plan` tool. From the force's tech tree and recipes, for a goal (technology or item; default the next science pack it can't make, then the rocket silo): the research path (prerequisites first, capped at 25), the science packs it takes, and machine/drill counts for `per_minute` of each pack (best unlocked machine, else the slowest still locked; never one that needs its own kind of machine's products to build when the engineer can't hand-craft that category: a steel furnace needs steel and bricks, so steel is smelted in a stone furnace, which waited all night once. Columns planned with such a furnace switch once, in `cells.build`). Skips recycling, barrel fill/empty and catalyst recipes; goal buildings aren't mass-produced. It's what the AI follows once keep-busy's build order and goals run out.
  - `scripts/goals.lua`: goals ("automate X at N/min") planned from the loaded game's recipes and tech tree, instead of hand-written steps. `resolve` is an AND/OR search over a recipe graph (any recipe for an item, all its ingredients, its unlocking research, a machine, a surface it works on) with cycle detection and reason codes (`NO_SOURCE`, `CYCLE`, `UNKNOWN_CAPABILITY`, `NO_VALID_SURFACE`, `RECIPE_LOCKED`). It takes `game_graph` (the force's real data) or `fixture_graph` (a made-up graph, used through the test-only `test_plan` op). Machines and cell parts only need their recipe unlocked (they're hand-crafted), else assemblers would need themselves. Iron and copper plates, coal, stone and wood are `SUPPLIED` (the base's), not expanded. A goal is stored with tasks (RESEARCH, POWER, SUPPLY, BUILD, VERIFY) whose ids are the goal's plus their purpose, so asking again returns the same goal; a blocked one is planned again. A keep-busy goal blocked by `EXECUTOR_ERROR` (jobs failing `MAX_FAILS` times) is taken up again where it was after 5 minutes, up to 3 times (`next_autonomous`, even while another goal runs), keeping its block and bus slot: a blocked bus goal held slot 0 forever and stopped research at the end of red science. Task status is read from the world every 2 s (`evaluate`), never set by a job running: VERIFY counts the goal's assemblers' `products_finished` and completes at 80% of the rate over a minute. Origins: `PLAYER` (the `goal` tool) runs with keep-busy on or off; `AUTONOMOUS` (keep-busy's, the next science pack in `LADDER` once the build order is done) pauses when keep-busy is turned off. Stop pauses all goals. Player goals emit `goal_done` / `goal_blocked`.
  - `scripts/cells.lua`: builds and runs goals: power (the build order's steam), research (lab, trigger crafts; packs other than red come from goals' chests first), then a column of cells (an assembler with its recipe, or a furnace, and a pole each, an inserter and chest beside each one making the goal's product) beside the power network, or on a pole line from it, kept clear of the plant's room and other goals' columns (with growth room south). The engineer feeds them by hand: other cells' output first, then plates and raw materials gathered like the build order does, each input filled to at most one stack (a furnace's slot, or 10-coal grenades). Complete goals keep being fed. Fluids and oil aren't built yet (the plan says so as UNKNOWN_CAPABILITY).
  - `scripts/bus.lua`: the main bus (the owner's base plan: ore lines, smelting, a bus, sub-factories off it). Once the ore lines are built and splitters researched, a goal's column goes on the bus (`cells.on_bus`, before the old placement by the power network): the first time, once everything for it is gathered (until the taps are there, the plate belts' end chests are the only place plates come out: removing them first deadlocked the engineer waiting for plates, so `build` puts them back if they're gone and the bus isn't built), the plate belts' end inserters and chests come out (emptied first), the belts turn in beside the coal belt at column X (rows 5, 6, 7 of the smelting area's frame) and run on east, with a tap (inserter into a chest, the new `plate_chests`) on each turned belt. Slot k (`SLOT_WIDTH` tiles each) has a splitter on each plate belt; the bottom plates go straight onto the west side of a feeder belt running south, the top ones over row 3 and under the bus by underground belt onto its east side. Fed from both sides and not from behind, the feeder's first tile puts each plate on its own lane whatever lanes they came on (with one straight input, copper and iron shared a lane and the copper never got through). The column's assemblers stand east of the feeder, each with an inserter from it (assemblers only take what their recipe uses) and a second pole on the west; furnace cells aren't belt-fed. Slots are taken in order and never freed (`s.bus_slots`; an untaken slot's splitter tiles get plain bus belt). Power runs along the bus on its own line of poles (`bus.power`: row 8, two to a slot, up to slot k), so a slot gets power when the ones before it aren't built; before that, slot 1 and up had no pole line while slot 0 was unbuilt (`bus.corridor`'s keep-clear rows can't be wired across) and the mall went off the bus, fed by hand. `bus.corridor` is the bus's exact parts, risers and every slot's sub-factory area, not one box. While a goal waits for plates to build with (`saving_plates`, renewed every 20 s), other goals' machines aren't fed plates, or hand-fed goals took them as fast as they were smelted. `bus.area` is in `planned_areas`, and site scoring for the smelting area prefers room for the first slots. The `place` step takes `belt_type` for underground belts.
  - **Between cells** (any column): `arrange` orders a new column's cells (swaps while more is covered) so most of what a cell needs comes from a neighbor (a link inserter in the gap) or down the **parts belt**: one belt 3 tiles east of the machines' centers, flowing south and dead-ending at the last cell taking from it, an inserter onto it in each maker's bottom row and one off it into each user's top row (powered by the pole above). One item per parts belt (`parts_for`: the one the most cells get from it): a dead-end belt with one kind of item fills up and its makers stop, where mixed items would jam. An output inserter whose chest spot the parts belt runs through becomes long-handed, its chest 4 tiles out (`settle`). The engineer only carries what neither the bus, a link nor the parts belt brings (`feed`), a trip's worth at a time, or whatever there is from a maker whose output is full (a gear cell that also feeds a neighbor by link stops at 3 gears, which never made a trip's worth). Inserters into machines are placed after the machines (one placed before grabs whatever passes and holds it), and `b.placed` keeps building going after BUILD (which counts machines) is complete.
  - **Labs by belt:** a column on the bus making red, green or military science gets a lane of the science belt (`science_lane`, `s.science_lanes`; south first, north only from slot 1, since its way round would hit the third lab): the packs go by long-handed inserter onto a riser belt 7 tiles east of the feeder, under the bus, and onto the science belt (row 1, flowing west) as a sideload, so each kind has its own lane. `bus.LABS` labs north of the bus's start take from it. Without room for that, the column falls back to a chest. With bus labs up, research and `keep_researching` fill bus lab 1 by hand only with packs not on the belt (`hand_lab`, `by_itself`). With the bus up, `next_autonomous` counts a pack as made only by a goal column, so keep-busy builds red science on the bus too.
  - **Nothing stays fed by hand** (the owner's rule: every assembler on belts and inserters as soon as it can be). Before the bus, keep-busy builds only one goal fed by hand (`next_autonomous`'s `wait_for_bus`, while the ore lines are planned or still being retried, up to 6 tries), and `more_science` adds no build-order science units once the ore lines are planned. Once the bus can be built, `cells.move_to_bus` (run by `decide` before any goal work, while no goal is being built) picks up each goal column that isn't on it (inserters first, chests emptied) and sets the goal ACTIVE again, so it's built on the bus; each goal at most once (`s.moved`) and only while a slot is free. With keep-busy, once the bus's red science is complete and reaches its labs by belt, the build order's red science units and their labs are picked up too (`science`, `science_gears` and `more_science` marked skipped so they aren't rebuilt).
  - **The mall** (`goals.add_mall`, the goal tool's item `mall`, keep-busy's ladder after green science once the bus is up): every `goals.MALL` item whose recipe is unlocked, planned together into one column, each product cell into its own chest (barred at `MALL_STACKS` stacks once built, so it stops instead of using up plates). Done once every item has come out. `get_items` takes what it needs from the mall's chests before hand-crafting.
  - **Furnace cells** (steel, bricks: smelting recipes of one ingredient, since a furnace picks its recipe from its input): the best unlocked furnace (`plan.machine_for`), placed by `goals.machine_pos` in the cell's lower right when it's 2x2, so the output inserter and a link inserter to the cell below still pick up from it (`links` checks both ends land on machines). A furnace only has a recipe while smelting, so `goals.cell_recipe` gives the recipe of the column cell it stands in (`block.machines`); `survey` keeps those out of the base's plate furnaces (no drill, no plate collecting) but in `burners`, so maintenance fuels them. BUILD counts burner machines as built without power (`goals.hooked_up`); VERIFY says `NO_FUEL` when they're out.
  - `scripts/pollution.lua`: the pollution cloud versus enemy nests (keep-busy). Every 30 s it measures, for each chunk with nests within 640 tiles of home, the chunk steps (pollution spreads to the four neighbors) to the nearest chunk over 1 pollution (the map overlay). It keeps `BUFFER` (2) chunks clear, one more on sparse land (trees per tile around home under `SPARSE_TREES`: desert spreads fastest) and one more before gun turrets are researched; `PREPARE` (2) chunks before that it's `prepare`, and a cloud that came a chunk closer since the last reading counts a chunk closer still. Trees and tiles soaking up pollution need no model: the cloud is measured. `reduce` pauses the engineer's drills with `disabled_by_script` (kept built, a yellow custom status says why): stone first, coal mine drills whose chest holds 200+, then iron and copper nearest the nest, keeping 2 iron and 1 copper running; 3 at a time (6 once the cloud reaches a nest), then waits `PAUSE_WAIT` unless the cloud still comes closer. Never boilers, coal loops or the owner's buildings. Clear for `RESUME_WAIT`, it restarts one drill per `RESUME_STEP`. Turning keep-busy off restarts all of them (`release`). Observe shows `pollution` (status words, chunks from the nest, kept clear, paused drills; just the status when clear).
  - `scripts/gui.lua`: the owner's chat window.
  - `scripts/frame.lua`: so a layout can face any way (the owner: nothing is tied to a compass direction). Positions and directions are worked out in the layout's own terms (x along, y across, as if running east with its side south) and turned or mirrored onto the map by a matrix `m` (8 frames; nil is as written). The bus's frame is `g.m` (`bus.at`, `bus.tile`, `bus.dir`, `bus.toward`, `bus.box`), a column's `b.m` (cells' `off` and `toward`, `goals.machine_pos`); a new column's parts-belt span is in tiles down the column (`span.along`; older saves' are map y). Nothing picks a frame other than east yet (stage 3 of the layout plan below). `test:bus-turned` builds on a turned bus.
  - `scripts/director.lua`: filming for the owner's videos. `/ai-cam` switches the owner to the spectator controller and eases the view onto the engineer every tick (`storage.cams`; the parked character is made indestructible and given back on a second `/ai-cam` or when they leave the game). `/ai-timelapse` renders frames off-screen with `game.take_screenshot` (`by_player`, so only the owner's machine writes them) into `script-output/ai-engineer-timelapse/` as JPEGs (`QUALITY`; PNGs were about 5 MB each): a follow shot (the default), a fixed wide shot, or both, each numbered on its own so ffmpeg's `%05d` has no gaps. The follow shot has `show_gui` on, so the chat window is in it. Headless tests can't render or have players, so check these in a real game.
  - **The terminal in the chat window:** the runner's `note` prints a line and sends it with the `log` op (`gui.note`: small grey text without a name, cut at `NOTE_MAX`), for viewers of recordings: tool calls, timings, cost, errors and what woke a turn. Lines the window already shows (owner messages, replies, progress notes) stay `log` only. Build order notes go to the window from the mod itself (`autopilot`'s `note`, unless `tell` already showed them), so they show with the app closed too.
  - `scripts/util.lua`: shared helpers.
- `app/src/`: the helper app (Bun + TypeScript, compiled to one executable).
  - `main.ts`: the command-line entry point and connect/pair loop.
  - `runner.ts`: polls the mod and decides when Claude takes a turn.
  - `agent.ts`: the Claude conversation and tool loop.
  - `tips.md`: how to play well plus Factorio tips (layouts, numbers, recipes), appended to the system prompt and embedded in the build. Write them for the engineer's own tools, keep them correct for 2.0, and keep them short (they're sent with every request). Changing them changes the system prompt, so existing conversations lose their cache once after an update.
  - `instance.ts`: one copy of the app per computer (it holds local port 47613).
  - `tools.ts`: tool schemas and validation.
  - `game.ts`: the typed `/aie` protocol.
  - `rcon.ts`: the RCON client.
  - `setup.ts`: the settings questions, asked at every start (explanations only the first time, the last answers as defaults), and Factorio config.ini editing.
  - `config.ts`: settings and the data folder.
- `scripts/`: the disposable-server test harness (`factorio.ts`), the real-game test, and mod install/package.

## Rules that matter

- **Owner isolation (Anthropic usage policy).** Each helper app is paired with one player (`storage.apps[app] = player index`), via a code shown in the app's terminal and typed with `/ai-pair`.
  - Only that player's `/ai` messages and chat-window input become `message` events for that app.
  - Never send other players' names, chat or text to Claude.
  - Replies go to the owner only: `player.print`, plus the owner's chat window.
- **`/aie` is RCON-only** (`cmd.player_index` must be nil). RCON access already allows any console command, so the RCON operations are trusted.
- **Test-only operations:** `test_spawn` / `test_message` / `test_keep_busy` create a player-less engineer (owner index 0) and stand in for owner messages and the keep-busy checkbox. They refuse to run on any server a player has joined. Owner 0 means "no player": guard `game.get_player` calls with `owner > 0`.
- **autopilot.lua is at Lua's limit of 200 local names in a chunk** ("too many local variables" when the mod loads). A new top-level helper goes on `M` or in a `do ... end` block with the code that uses it (the ore lines' planning steps are), and constants go inside the function using them.
- **One tool list in two places.** `app/src/tools.ts` (the schemas Claude sees) and `mod/scripts/tools.lua` (the handlers). `bun run test:game` fails if the names differ.
- **Cost comes first.** Every response is paid from the player's API key, and it's the main thing that decides whether anyone keeps this mod running. Prefer changes that let one response do more (the `steps` job) over ones that add turns.
- **Jobs:** one job per engineer, and a new job replaces the old one. The agent starts at most one job per response (`JOB_TOOLS`; extra job calls get an error result), and a started job ends the turn without asking the model again, unless an owner message arrived meanwhile. That saves the paid response that would only end the turn, and stops small local models from starting jobs that replace each other. The next input then follows the tool results as a second user message in a row. `steps` chains up to 20 jobs into one, reported with a single event (a place/insert step waits for hand-crafting). A step still going after `STEP_TICKS` (3 minutes) ends the job, saying where the engineer was, what it walked to and what's queued; the build order tells the owner each time ("Stopped a job that seemed stuck"). A pickup's failure says why (full inventory, something else selected, or how far mining got), and `repair` gives up on a drill after `MAX_FAILS` failed pickups. `remind_me` sets one timer per engineer that emits a `reminder` event, so the AI can wake itself while waiting with no job running.
  - Game chat stays quiet: text from a turn woken by events that just started the next job or reminder is shown as a progress note (chat window only). When automatic turns run out, the owner is told once. Jobs report back through `util.emit` events (`job_done`, `job_failed`, `crafting_done`, `engineer_died`, …).
  - The runner wakes the AI on events, but only up to `AUTO_TURNS` (12) times per owner message, which keeps unattended spending bounded. Claude in keep-busy and local models are unlimited: the owner wants a free local model to keep at a task until it's done or they say stop (12 turns cut "make 100 ammo" off repeatedly). It once wandered off for a whole night chaining its own turns, so it's still never woken by idle nudges, only by its own jobs and reminders. `crafting_done` during a running job doesn't wake it (it rides along with the job's event), and turns woken only by `job_done`/`crafting_done` skip the automatic observe.
  - **Defense** (keep-busy on, right after maintenance): control.lua's `on_entity_died` (filtered to our building types and characters) calls `autopilot.attacked` when the enemy force destroys something of ours within 200 tiles of home; `defend` puts two gun turrets there (researching gun-turret first if needed) and keeps every turret of ours at `AMMO_FILL` magazines. Before any attack, while pollution is within `prepare` of a nest: `turrets_first` puts gun-turret at the front of the research queue (also when attacked; a switch run every loop, even while the engineer is busy), two turrets go up by the engineer's building nearest that nest, up to 10 tiles toward it but outside `NEST_CLEARANCE`, and `AMMO_RESERVE` magazines are kept on hand. New turrets go up as soon as they're researched with `AMMO_START` (2) magazines each, and are topped up to `AMMO_FILL` afterwards from what's on hand, ammo made `AMMO_BATCH` at a time, so making ammo never holds them up. While the cloud is too close or drills are paused (`pollution.limited`), keep-busy builds no growth drills, coal mine, power or autonomous goals (the owner's goals still run), and poll's `autopilot` stays true so the AI isn't woken to build more. Boilers are refueled sooner and fuller than other burners (`BOILER_LOW`/`BOILER_FILL`), except boilers an inserter feeds off the coal belt (`survey`'s `belt_fed`): an inserter keeps only a few coal in one, always under `BOILER_LOW`, so they were topped up by hand forever; they're refueled by hand only once empty.
  - **Storage** (`scripts/stash.lua`, keep-busy on, right after `repair`): below `MIN_FREE` (6) empty slots, what the engineer carries but doesn't use (`KEEP`: all iron and copper ore; stone, wood and coal past 100/100/200; burner drills once the coal belt runs; stone furnaces past 10 once the ore lines are done) goes into iron chests near home (`s.stash`), beside the last one, out of `planned_areas`, the plant's growth strip and goals' columns, with nothing but other storage chests next to it (an inserter there would take from it). Once started it goes on until all of it is away, a new chest as one fills. `get_items` takes from the storage chests (and the mall's) before crafting or mining. `test:stash` covers it.
  - **Coal mine** (`coal_mine`): electric drills on coal each filling a chest, on a pole line from the network, two per boiler the coal belt doesn't feed (all of them when the belt couldn't be built); `get_coal` takes from those chests first.
  - **No playbook**: the step-by-step plan that used to be in the system prompt is gone (small models followed it as orders, and it would never be finished). Progression is the mod's: keep-busy's build order, then goals planned from the game's own recipes and tech tree. How-to for the AI goes in `tips.md`.
  - **After the build order** (keep-busy on), in this order each decision: grow the power plant once the engines run above `GROW_LOAD` (a second engine on the first boiler, then boiler rows piped in series, each the first spot 4-7 tiles behind the last that fits, with poles past each row, inside `power.plant`'s room; footprints are rotated by direction; someone standing there doesn't count; a pole of ours in the way, e.g. the coal belt's on the water line, is moved within 2 tiles first, keeping what it powers and its wires (`pole_swap`); otherwise retried every 5 minutes), keep-busy's own goal (the next science pack), `cells.keep_researching` (the tech tree in order: lowest pack tier, then cheapest; only packs the engineer's assemblers make; green and later packs fed to the lab from the goals' chests), then `upgrade` (each burner drill/furnace pair on iron or copper, and the stone drill into a chest, replaced by an electric drill feeding a furnace or chest with a pole, built first and the old pair picked up after, only while the engines are under `MAX_LOAD`). `survey` counts electric drill/furnace pairs as pairs, so growth doesn't rebuild burner ones.
  - **No burner drills once the coal belt runs:** `repair` picks up the coal loops (coal comes off the belt: `get_coal` takes from the belt by the coal drills first, and `take` with an item at a belt takes it off the belts in reach); growth goals build electric pairs (`build_electric`), and a growth drill waiting for power doesn't hold up the later steps (power growth is one). The actual order after the build order is: `feed_boilers` (every boiler gets an inserter off the coal belt, rows built before the belt included, and the belt an electric drill per boiler, `M.belt_drill_spot`: beside a belt tile over coal, else the belt made up to 12 tiles longer straight back from its start, which often sits at the coal's edge, with the coal mine's drills, chests and poles in the way moved by `clear_way`; retried every 5 minutes; `coal_mine` only covers boilers the belt can't), `coal_mine`, `more_power` (while pollution holds back building, only engines, no boilers), `upgrade`, `radar` (one radar, once its recipe is enabled, by the network pole nearest the middle of the engineer's buildings), keep-busy's own goals, then research.
  - **The plant's growth strip** (`power.growth_area`: the room plus `GROWTH` tiles further from the water): the lab line, science, goals' columns, electric pairs and the radar stay out of it. The lab's pole line runs straight along an axis, to the sides of the growth direction first. The coal belt comes in along the fuel lane through the whole strip (`power.grown`; falling back to just the room, with a build-log note, when that can't be routed), its poles stay out of the room but for the lane, and its route counts `TURN` belts per turn so it runs in straight lines (`coal_line_plan` is a Dijkstra over tile and belt direction). `cells.pole_line` runs as an L of two straight lines where it can.
  - **Ore lines** (`ore_lines`, `lines_plan`; before `upgrade`, which then only swaps the stone drill, or iron and copper pairs when the lines couldn't be laid out): `ORE_LINES` electric drills (4 iron, 2 copper) plus `LINE_COAL_DRILLS` (1) on coal, each field a straight belt with drills either side facing it, 4 tiles apart with a pole in each gap (`field_plan`), its belt routed (`belt_route`: the same turn-counting search over tile and belt direction as the coal belt, but A* with the tile distance weighed twice, after a flood fill that finds an impossible route in milliseconds and keeps the search to tiles the start can reach) to a smelting area (`smelter_layout`, the owner's design, in the bus's frame: a **trunk** of three belts (column A's ore, column B's ore, coal; rows -5, -2, 1, two rows apart so a branch's underground belts fit between them) from column 0, routes ending at column -1 going along it; **smelting columns** hanging off it across the bus, each fed by three branches (a splitter each, under the lower trunk rows and the bus by underground belt): ore down the middle (both lanes, never shared with coal: that would bottleneck), a coal belt down each outer edge, furnaces both sides with an inserter from the middle, one onto the plate belt and a long-handed one over it for coal, a gap row of poles every two furnaces a side; plate belts flow back up, merge at the head one side per lane and rise onto the **bus**: column A's plates on row 5, coal on 6 (a U-turn up from under the bus between the columns: the bus carries everything but ore), column B's on 7, each row starting where its source joins so nothing crosses; a tap on each plate row (the plate chests from the start; `bus.base` adds and removes nothing for `frame.columns`). Columns grow by getting longer (`GROW` rows kept, routes stay out, `inner`). `test:smelter` places it in all eight frames and runs ore through it. Saves planned before keep their layout: the turned block (`frame.turned`) or the old east-running rows, each plate belt ending in an inserter into a chest). The site is clear ground (`area_clear`: no water, ore or buildings) near the middle of the fields, outside the plant's growth strip, goals' columns and the science layout. **Which way it faces is chosen** (`sites_step` tries all eight frames, `bus.FRAMES`, `work.f`, at every spot): each is scored also by the ground under the bus it would lead to (`bus_ground` over `bus.area`: water and ore tiles, cliffs heavily, trees lightly, other buildings, chunks not charted (or not generated) as risk, and the nearest known nest within 300 tiles heavily, the nearer the worse). The frame goes into the plan (`plan.frame`) and never changes. Copper is column A instead (`flip`) when that keeps the belts from crossing: all three come in at the trunk's start, so sites are scored by whether the fields lie in the trunk rows' order seen from there, and fields past the start cost more (in the frame's terms, `bus.where`); the 8 best sites (at most two per frame, or they're near-copies that all fail the same way) are routed, each in two orders. The pole line from the network goes to the layout's pole nearest the network. The ore lines wait for splitters and underground belts. ## Next to do: a layout that follows the terrain

The owner wants this to work on any map seed: everything built facing away from alien bases, dense trees and cliffs, chosen from the map rather than fixed. The direction is now chosen when the ore lines are planned (steps 2-4 below are done; see the ore lines above). **Stage 4, growing with demand**, is in (`M.grow_smelting`, after `upgrade` in the build order's later steps; `M.smelting_sample` every autopilot tick): every 10 seconds each column's furnaces' statuses are counted; over 5 minutes, a column working ≥85% of the time with ≤10% output full gets two more furnaces (`smelter_layout` again with the column two longer, kept within `layout.room`, what isn't there yet built), one ≥30% short of ore a drill more. Each field keeps the drills its column needs (`needed`: furnaces × plate rate ÷ drill rate, coal for every furnace with half again to spare), its line longer upstream two drills at a time (`field_more`, from `plan.fields[ore]`, the line `field_plan` found); drills on ore that ran out are picked up (their poles stay). A field that can't get longer gets a drill beside its belt further down where that still crosses the ore (`line_belts` follows it to the trunk's first splitter, then `M.belt_drill_spot`: a coal field placed at the coal's edge had its route leave across the rest of it), else is tried again every 5 minutes (`s.field_retry_at`; said once). A column out of room is said once and not grown again (`s.grow_blocked`). Plans store `layout` and `fields` for this; older plans don't grow. Not yet: more trunk belts (a column can grow to ~26 furnaces in its growth rows, about half a yellow belt, so the trunk isn't the limit until a second column per ore is needed), and a new field when a line can't get longer. Then **stage 5**: steel and brick columns and stone on the trunk, which needs a bus with more rows (today it has three: plates, coal, plates). There are 8 slots at fixed offsets (`bus.SLOTS`, `bus.SLOT_WIDTH`). The only adaptive parts:
- The ore-lines planner scores smelting sites, preferring room for the first slots.
- A goal takes the first free slot whose column is clear (water, cliffs, ore and buildings block; trees and rocks are chopped).
- With no slot left, a goal is built off the bus near the power network and fed by hand.

On a seed with water, cliffs or ore east or south of the smelting area, slots get skipped until everything is hand-fed.

What's already there to build on:
- Nearly every bus position goes through `bus.tile(g, tx, ty)`: tiles along the bus and to its side, from the frame `bus.geometry` makes.
- Nests are known from pollution handling and scouting (`pollution.lua`).
- Planning already runs in steps across ticks (`lines_work`), so scoring several layouts won't hitch.

In order:
1. **Side per slot:** each sub-factory branches to whichever side of the bus is clearer (north or south). Smallest step; it alone would have kept the mall out of the forest. The labs and science belt north of the bus have to move or be avoided, and the riser (`bus.riser`) and slot parts (`bus.slot`) need a mirrored version.
2. **A rotatable bus frame:** `bus.geometry` gets a direction (and a side), and `bus.tile` turns `tx, ty` through it. Every belt, splitter, underground and inserter direction in `bus.lua` and `cells.lua` (`east_inserter`, `inserter_toward`, column cells) turns with it, not just positions.
3. **A rotatable smelting area:** its plate exit faces the way the bus runs. Its layout is in `autopilot.lua` (`smelter_layout`), which is at Lua's 200-local limit, so new code goes on `M` or in a `do` block, or the smelting layout moves to its own module.
4. **Choosing the layout:** when the ore lines are planned, score each direction and side over the area the layout would take up. Pick the best and keep it in `storage` (with the ore plan): a built bus can't change direction without tearing the factory down.
   - Nests: heavy penalty, the nearer the worse. Unscouted land in that direction counts as risk (or scout it first).
   - Cliffs: heavy penalty. They block building until cliff explosives, much later.
   - Water and ore: count as blocked tiles.
   - Dense trees: a smaller penalty (chopping costs time, and the wood fills the inventory).
5. **Later: bending round obstacles.** The bus turns a corner, or a second bus starts, when it meets cliffs or water. Even a well-chosen straight bus eventually runs into something.

Caveats:
- Only generated or charted land can be judged.
- Existing saves keep their east/south layout: the frame defaults to it when a save has none.
- Tests need maps with nests, cliffs, water and forest placed in different directions, to check the engineer turns away from each. Today's tests use fixed seeds or flat maps.

Do 1 on its own first. Do 2-4 together: a rotated bus only works once the smelting area turns with it.
